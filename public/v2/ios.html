Did it work
Palette

This carries the web revamp's language — dark green ground, the modelled marks. The shipping iOS app is still on the older mobile palette (#08080f near-black, #00e5a0 mint). Two surfaces, two looks; this is the argument for which one wins.

Same numbers as the web

The score, the ceiling, all seven pillars and the whole day are copied from the web mockup, not re-typed (formulate-dense/s/95-script-a.html). 67.2 and 77.3 are the weighted means of the pillar table, computed here the same way — nothing on a screen states a score, it derives one. A phone saying 69 while the web says 67 is two products.

Seven pillars, not six

This follows the web app, which still carries Nutrition. You asked to fold it away on the landing page, and that is still the right call for the copy — but dropping the weight reprices every existing score, and it would make this mockup disagree with the app it is meant to match. One data edit in 15-data.html either way; it is a product decision, not a tidy-up.

Why the catalog needs two levels

It is not five categories — it is 12 catalogs across five pillars, and two of the pillars are four and five catalogs deep on their own: Diet is whole foods, recipes, meals and pantry (1,265); Personal care is skin, body, hair, oral and fragrance (1,166). A flat chip row was never the right shape however small the chips got. So the search row carries pillars, and a pillar opens its own catalogs into that same row rather than into a second one. Counts read from formulate-web/src/data, each file's own item list — summing every array in them inflated Personal care by 311. Sleep's 552 are built but sitting on a branch, so the screen marks them rather than counting them as live.

Where sharing happens

It didn't, until now — there was a share glyph on a product wired to nothing. Your record is the thing you share: You › Your public record, or the share glyph, both land there. What makes it worth reading is the middle section, straight from the web app: what changed — and what it did, not what it was meant to do. One of the three rows says a change did nothing, and leaving it there is the only reason the other two are believable. The second half is the privacy line, on the screen instead of in a settings page: four of six switches on, and blood panel numbers off — direction only, never the value.

Six doors, one avatar

The web app has six: Today, Record, Catalog, Learning, Community, You. All six are here. You is the avatar, top right, with a dropdown — Account, Testing, your public record, Settings — which is how a phone does it and buys back a tab. The other tab came from deleting My Stack: on the web that is not a door at all, it is Today's second segment, and the phone already had that toggle, so the tab was a second door to a room that already had one. Test it moved with You, to Testing, where the web keeps it.

Ask, not Companion

The Companion tab is gone and Ask has it. The web app's AI is not a place you visit — it is a bar that knows which screen you are on and offers the question that screen makes you want to ask, with a caption naming what it would answer from. So the phone gets both halves: the bar sits on the tab bar on every screen and travels with you, and the tab is where it opens to. The answer there is three rows out of your own log with their point values, adding to exactly the ten the home screen promises — an assistant that cannot show its working is a search box with opinions. The build fails if any screen loses its bar, or if the bar starts saying the same thing everywhere.

Colour means something

Three colours, each answering one question, the same on every screen: green what you logged, orange what this stack is worth taken as written, blue what it is worth once improved. Today is green and orange only. Its meter draws two lengths — what you logged, and the gap from there to potential — and “on the table” is that gap rather than a third thing, so it is said in the line beside the ring instead of getting a number of its own. Blue is only ever the ceiling, and the ceiling only lives on My Stack, because today cannot reach it — where all three sit on one ring: green out to what you logged, orange on to potential, blue on to the ceiling. Amber stays what it always was — missed, overdue, slipping — and is deliberately not one of the three. The build measures all three against the ground and fails if one drops under 3:1 or two become the same colour.

The ceiling is new

Logged and potential are the web app's numbers. Ceiling is not in the web app yet — it is this mockup's proposal: what the same stack gives once improved, released one improvement a day. It is deliberately only +4.8, and every one of the seven is free. A ceiling promising twenty points is an invitation to rebuild your stack in an afternoon and abandon it inside a week. The build fails if any pillar's ceiling has no improvement queued behind it, or if the total grows past what a week can pace.

Real, and not

Real: the pillar scores, the day's windows, the products, brands, photos and catalog scores, the 40/35/25 component weights, and every lab range on “Test it” (from lib/biomarkers.ts). Sample: the person — whose stack and blood values these are.

Run it on a phone

Add ?live=1 to this page's address and the frames drop away: one screen fills the whole viewport and the tab bar switches between them. Today is one screen there and simply scrolls — the three phones in the rail above are the same screen at three scroll positions, which is the only honest way to show a screen this long on a desktop. That is the version to open on the iPhone.
